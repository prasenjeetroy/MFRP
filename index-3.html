A THOUGHT TO TRY

Why does sine repeat?

A point moving around a circle keeps returning to the same height. That height is sin(x)—so the wave is a circle, seen from the side.

CALCULUS · 6 MIN

What does a derivative really measure?

Zoom in on a curve until it looks like a straight line. The slope you see is the rate of change, right there, right now.

See the idea
For a function f, the derivative at a is the limit of the average slopes nearby: f′(a) = limh→0 [f(a+h) − f(a)] / h. For f(x) = x², that local slope is 2x.

GEOMETRY · 8 MIN

Why is π hiding in every circle?

Measure any circle’s edge and divide by its width. Big circle or tiny one, you’ll always find the same curious number.

See the idea
Circumference scales in exactly the same way as diameter. Their ratio is constant: C / d = π. So every circle has C = 2πr, and its area is A = πr².
03?

PROBABILITY · 5 MIN

Can a streak still be random?

Our brains love patterns—even when coin flips are doing their own wonderfully unpredictable thing.

See the idea
For a fair coin, each flip has a ½ chance of heads regardless of the flips before it. A run of five heads is unusual, but not evidence that the next flip must be tails.
